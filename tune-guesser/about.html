Name the record.

Tune Guesser drops you into the middle of a record and gives you four choices. Get it right and the next one comes up. Get one wrong and you're back to record 1. The leaderboard counts the longest run of right answers in a row.

How it works

Records 1–5 are picture sleeves: every pad shows the title and the artist.

From record 6 you're on white labels: the pads show only the artist, the way an unmarked promo has nothing on it but a name in marker. The records also get more obscure as you go, from big crossover hits to deep cuts.

You get three hints per run, one use each. 50:50 removes two wrong answers. Year shows when the record came out. Song name puts the titles back on the pads, and only works on white labels.

There's no clock. Listen for as long as you like.

The crate

207 foundational house, techno and drum & bass records, from Detroit electro in 1981 through to the late 2000s. Nothing released after 2010. That covers Chicago house and acid, Detroit techno, UK rave and bleep, jungle, French house and the vocal anthems.

After every answer the printer gives you a few lines on what the record is, who made it and why it matters.

Where the music comes from

Every track streams from YouTube, and only from the artist's own official channel or a YouTube "Topic" channel. Topic channels are the auto-generated ones that labels and distributors feed with official releases. No fan uploads, and no rips.

Each printout links straight to the video it played, so you can go and hear the whole thing.

Some classics aren't in the crate because no official upload of them exists.
