A T-shirt investigation

The Parody
Index

Some album art is so simple that strangers can't stop rewriting it. I checked 1,524 T-shirts to see which icon gets the most jokes.

By Todd L. Burns · October 2026
Scroll ↓

Earlier this month I made Known Pleasures, a wall of T-shirts that rework the cover of Joy Division's Unknown Pleasures. The waves turn into mountains, bacon, Garfield, a cat knocking things off a table. I stopped at 142.

That made me wonder whether Unknown Pleasures is special, or whether every famous band image gets this treatment. So I took sixteen of the most parodied icons in rock and rap, searched TeePublic for each one, and looked at every result by eye. A shirt counted only if it made a joke out of the original. Straight bootlegs didn't count.

Start with the one I already knew. Using the same quick method as every other icon here, Unknown Pleasures turns up 21 parodies.

When I dug for days, I found 142. So read every number on this page as a floor, not a ceiling.

Now the other fifteen. Unknown Pleasures lands in the middle of the pack, at number eight.

The top three are a crosswalk, a prism and a lightning bolt.

Abbey Road wins with 70: Santas, Simpsons, ducks, Peanuts, the Golden Girls, four coffee pots. Close behind are The Dark Side of the Moon (60) and the AC/DC logo (53).

Some of the most famous images barely get touched. These four came up nearly empty on the first pass, so I searched again more broadly. Even then, the Nirvana smiley produced only 9 jokes and London Calling 8.

Nirvana has gone to court over a smiley riff before: it sued Marc Jacobs in 2018.

Every parody does one of three things.

Swap the words keeps the font and changes the letters. Swap the cast keeps the scene and changes who's in it. Swap the object keeps the shape and changes what it is.

The kind of joke matters more when you look at hit rate: how much of what you find is actually a parody.

Search for a Misfits or AC/DC parody and more than 8 in 10 results are jokes. For the Stones tongue, Steal Your Face and the Nirvana smiley, it's under 1 in 10. The rest are the logo, straight.

Icons with a slot to fill, like a cast or a word, get rewritten. Icons that are just one picture mostly get copied.

Four Beatles are four slots. The Stones tongue is just the tongue.

How this was made

  • All shirts come from TeePublic, searched on October 21, 2026. I ran two searches per icon, such as "black flag parody" and "black flag bars parody", and took the first two pages of each.
  • The four icons with the fewest hits (Nirvana smiley, London Calling, Steal Your Face and the Stones tongue) got three extra, broader searches each. They still came last, so the extra digging only helps them.
  • Every result was checked by eye. A shirt counts if it's recognisably the original with a joke swapped in. Straight bootlegs, tributes, tour shirts and flag recolours don't count.
  • TeePublic's own result counts are noisy. "Dark side of the moon parody" returns 608 results, and I found 60 parodies in the 122 I could see. The table above shows both numbers.
  • Redbubble's search was too loose to count (it returns 11,387 results for "black flag parody"), so it's left out.
  • Every count is a floor. Known Pleasures found 142 Unknown Pleasures shirts by digging; the method here found 21.
